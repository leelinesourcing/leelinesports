# Phase 3: Copywriting Guide

Derived from the Landing Page Writing Methodology. Every section's copy must pass all rules below. This guide is the definitive reference for producing B2B landing page copy that is zero-pollution, evidence-driven, buyer-question-aligned, and competitively differentiated.

---

## Part 1: Overview & Core Principles

Great B2B copy is not about showing "how great we are." It's about becoming the buyer's **risk-control solution**. B2B purchasing is fundamentally risk management. Buyers care about:

- You understand my industry pain (strong resonance)
- You have a concrete solution with specific parameters (clear plan)
- You have proven precedents + verifiable evidence (trust)
- **Your information is unique and cannot be directly copied by competitors** (information gain)

### The 3-Second Rule
A visitor must understand within 3 seconds: "What problem do you solve for me, what specific benefit do I get, and how do I start with zero risk?"

### Core Principles (applied uniformly throughout)

- **Client-centric**: Every sentence answers "What specific benefit do I get? Which pain of mine is precisely solved?"
- **Emotion + Logic + Evidence**: First trigger emotion with a real scenario, then back it with specific numbers + verifiable actions + real people + visual evidence.
- **Scannable + High-density**: Short paragraphs, big numbers, bullet lists, bold headers. Zero tolerance for sentences that "still work if you swap the company name with any competitor."
- **Competitive differentiation first**: Use real details, real scenarios, and the buyer's own language. Content must pass competitive calibration — highlight your unique information gain.

### The Universal Three-Part Formula
Every section and claim follows this pattern:

1. **Pain Resonance**: [Industry role] + [specific frequent scenario] + [emotional pain point]
2. **Solution Visualization**: Action-first + quantified promise + specific parameters/process + process visualization
3. **Trust Endorsement**: Named client/anonymous major client + quantified result + time anchor + verifiable certification/real person endorsement

## Part 2: 5-Step Content Optimization SOP

Before writing or rewriting any landing page, execute these 5 steps. This SOP compresses 30-45 minutes of manual work to ~10 minutes (with AI assistance).

### Step 1: Zero Information Pollution — Purge the Fluff

Scan the copy and delete every sentence in these 3 categories on sight. This is purification, not deletion — information density naturally rises after cleanup.

**Category A — Generic Praise** (adjectives any B2B service/product can use):
- "high quality", "superior quality", "professional team", "experienced experts", "reliable partner", "cost-effective solution", "competitive pricing", "best service"

**Category B — Industry-Stacking Fluff** (vague application descriptions):
- "widely used in various industries", "suitable for many applications", "helps businesses grow", "widely applied in manufacturing, energy, etc."

**Category C — Empty Promises** (unverifiable guarantees):
- "customer satisfaction is our top priority", "we provide the best service", "timely delivery guaranteed", "100% quality assurance", "we are committed to excellence"

**Before**:
> "Our team consists of experienced professionals using advanced equipment and strict quality control processes to ensure high-quality, reliable service and timely delivery."

**After (evidence-escalated)**:
> "Our butterfly valves are cast from CF8M stainless steel. Every unit undergoes hydrostatic testing at 1.5x rated pressure before shipping — a standard continuously applied since 2018. In the past 12 months, our QC team identified and rejected 340 units with micro-porosity defects (test photos below). The process is overseen by our senior valve engineer with 11 years of industrial fluid control experience."

**Why this works** (learn this thought process):
- CF8M → material grade, professional signal
- 1.5x rated pressure → specific test standard, verifiable
- Since 2018 → time anchor, routine process, not ad-hoc
- Rejected 340 units → counterintuitive but authentic: proves QC is real (never rejecting anything is suspicious)
- Test photos → directs to physical evidence
- 11-year senior engineer → real person endorsement

Apply this same standard to every module: Hero, Pain Points, What We Deliver, Our Team, Real Results, Why We're Different, etc.

**Zero-Pollution Quick Check**:
- [ ] No instance of "quality" as an unqualified descriptor
- [ ] No instance of "professional" describing the team
- [ ] No "various", "wide range", "comprehensive" without specifics
- [ ] No "customer satisfaction" claim without evidence
- [ ] No "leading" unless third-party verified (e.g., "G2 Leader 2024")
- [ ] Every claim has a specific referent (company, number, person, standard)

### Step 2: Buyer Question-Driven Content

After purging pollution, fill back in with content that answers the buyer's real questions. Map these 8 canonical questions to your sections:

1. What specific pain in my current scenario does this solve? (→ Hero, Pain Points — give 1-2 concrete scenarios + quantified impact)
2. What are the core deliverables, timeline, cost range, or MOQ? (→ What We Deliver, How It Works)
3. What international/industry certifications, methodologies, or standards back this? (→ Hero, Why Different, Trust Bar)
4. Which specific industries/scenarios does this apply to? (→ Who This Is For, Real Results — 1-2 client profiles + results, never "all industries")
5. Compared to competitors, in which measurable dimensions are you better? (→ Why We're Different — use data or unique evidence)
6. How do I start? How quickly do I get a quote/first deliverable/see initial results? (→ How It Works, CTA — low-risk entry)
7. If problems or changes arise, how do you handle them? (→ FAQ — SLA, response time, remediation)
8. What verifiable results have similar clients achieved? (→ Real Results — with numbers + timeline + evidence type)

### Step 3: Competitive Calibration — Find Information Gain

After filling the buyer question checklist, run a competitive content gap analysis:

1. Search your target keywords and open the top 2-3 competitor landing pages.
2. Compare point by point: which buyer questions did they answer that you haven't? Fill those gaps.
3. Finally ask: **Is there information that competitors didn't write, but your buyers genuinely care about, and you have real data/cases to support?**
   - This is your **information gain** — unique content that is your real weapon to outrank and out-convert competitors.

**Common gap areas for service businesses**:
- Specific process timelines + deliverables per phase + what the client can do during each phase
- Real rejection/problem cases + closed-loop evidence of resolution (instead of just saying "strict control")
- Industry/scenario-specific customization parameters or case library
- Transparent open-book costing or milestone payments tied to verification
- Individual team member real endorsements (instead of generic company self-praise)

### Step 4: Information Density Audit

After fixing content, count words in core modules (Hero + Pain + Deliver + Results + Why Different):

| Word Count | Verdict | Action |
|------------|---------|--------|
| 800+ with high-density specifics | Density sufficient | Verify industry terms / entity signals aligned |
| 400-800 | Moderate | Supplement with specific specs/parameters, certification details, use case scenarios, quantified evidence |
| Below 400 | Page is nearly invisible to Google and users | Rewrite seriously. Word count is the result, not the starting point — add valuable information line by line and count will follow |

**High-density standard**: Every paragraph/bullet contains at least one of: specific number, parameter, time anchor, real person/real case endorsement, or verifiable action.

## Part 3: Evidence Escalation Formula (5-Part)

Every major claim must follow this structure:

```
Specific Action + Number + Time Anchor + Real Person + Verifiable Evidence
```

### Example

| Bad | Good |
|-----|------|
| "We deliver high-quality results" | "Reduced inventory reconciliation time from 14 hours to 45 minutes per week for Maplewood Manufacturing — verified by their Q2 2024 audit report." |

### Evidence Hierarchy (use highest available level)

1. **Third-party verification**: Audit reports, certification bodies, press mentions
2. **Named client with numbers**: "Acme Corp reduced downtime by 37% in 6 months"
3. **Composite client with numbers**: "Across 200+ deployments, average ROI is 4.2x in year one"
4. **Internal metrics**: "Our support team resolves 92% of tickets within 4 hours"
5. **Founder/team credentials**: "Led engineering at SpaceX for 8 years"

### Evidence Requirement per Section

| Section | Minimum Evidence Required |
|---------|--------------------------|
| Hero | 1 trust signal (client count, years, certification) |
| Pain Points | 1 quantified pain scenario with real numbers |
| What We Deliver | 1 quantified outcome per offering |
| How It Works | Time per step + deliverable description |
| Real Results | 1-2 full case studies with named/described client |
| Why We're Different | 1 verifiable differentiator with data |
| What Clients Say | 1-2 testimonials with attribution |
| Final CTA | 1 urgency or scarcity signal |

## Part 4: Three-Part Section Formula

Each content section (excluding Hero and FAQ) follows:

```
Pain Resonance → Solution Visualization (with parameters) → Trust Endorsement
```

### Part 1: Pain Resonance (1-2 sentences)
- Name a specific frustration the buyer has
- Use their language (from interview buyer questions)
- *Not*: "Many businesses struggle with inefficiency"
- *Yes*: "You're spending 12 hours a week manually entering data from three different systems. One typo and the monthly report is wrong."

### Part 2: Solution Visualization (2-3 sentences)
- Show how the problem is solved with parameters
- Include specific numbers, timelines, or process steps
- *Not*: "Our solution streamlines your workflow"
- *Yes*: "Our API connects your ERP, CRM, and warehouse system in under 2 weeks. Data flows in real time. Your team reviews — they don't re-enter."

### Part 3: Trust Endorsement (1 sentence)
- A verifiable proof point
- *Not*: "Thousands of customers trust us"
- *Yes*: "Used by 47 manufacturing plants in the Midwest since 2019."

## Part 5: Title & H1 Optimization

Title (what appears in search results) and H1 (what appears at the top of the page) serve different purposes and must be optimized separately.

### Title Tag Formula (SERP — decides whether to click)

```
Core Keyword + Differentiating Information + Credibility Anchor
```

| Core Keyword | Differentiator | Credibility Anchor |
|-------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Inventory reconciliation | Real-time, multi-system | Used by 47 plants |
| Compliance training | Role-specific, modular | ISO 9001:2015 aligned |
| China sourcing partner | Pre-vetted factories + full QC loop | 340+ rejected batches documented (2024) |

**How to research Titles**:
1. Google your target keywords, copy the top 5 competitor Titles, and compare their differentiation and trust signals.
2. Use Semrush (free tier: 10 searches/day) to see Related Keywords / Modifiers that real buyers use (e.g., "food grade", "high pressure", "transparent pricing").
3. Use AI to batch-generate candidates (see Prompt 5 in Part 2).

**Length validation**: After writing, use a SERP preview tool or Google search preview to confirm the title isn't truncated (ideal: 50-60 characters for English).

### H1 Formula (Page header — confirms "you're in the right place")

```
[Emotional Resonance]: [Specific Outcome] for [Target Audience]
```

H1 can and should differ from Title. Title makes users "stop and click." H1 makes them "confirm this is the answer" upon landing.

**Example pair**:
- **Title**: "Reduce Hidden Supply Chain Costs 18-25% | ISO 9001 + 98% On-Time for Scaling Brands"
- **H1**: "Supply Chain Costs Don't Have to Be Your Biggest Risk — We Use Verifiable Closed-Loop Processes to Minimize It for Growing Brands"

**H1 formula**: "Inventory Reconciliation Software: Cut Manual Work from 12 Hours to 45 Minutes for Manufacturing Ops Teams"

### Meta Description Formula (SERP — the ad copy beneath your Title)

```
Core Keyword + Specific Value Proposition + Differentiator + Low-Risk CTA
```

The meta description doesn't directly affect rankings, but it's your **free ad copy** in the SERP — it directly controls click-through rate. A weak description loses the click even if you rank #1.

**Length**: 150-160 characters for English. Google may dynamically rewrite longer descriptions, so keep the key message within the first 150 characters.

| Component | Purpose | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Core Keyword | Confirms relevance to the query | "Supply chain cost reduction" |
| Specific Value Proposition | Answers "what do I get?" with numbers | "Cut hidden logistics costs 18-25%" |
| Differentiator | Answers "why you vs. competitors?" | "3-stage QC inspection with photo evidence" |
| Low-Risk CTA | Removes friction to click | "Free diagnostic in 24 hours" |

**Full example**:
- **Title**: "Reduce Hidden Supply Chain Costs 18-25% | ISO 9001 + 98% On-Time for Scaling Brands"
- **Meta Description**: "Identify and eliminate hidden logistics costs eating your margins. 3-stage supplier inspection with photo evidence at every stage. Get a free supply chain diagnostic within 24 hours — no obligation."
- **H1**: "Supply Chain Costs Don't Have to Be Your Biggest Risk — We Use Verifiable Closed-Loop Processes to Minimize It for Growing Brands"

**Meta Description Rules**:
1. **Must** include at least one specific number or quantified outcome (not "save money" but "cut costs 18-25%")
2. **Must** include one differentiator that competitors don't claim (from your competitive calibration in Part 2, Step 3)
3. **Must** end with a low-risk action signal ("free", "within X hours", "no obligation", "customized")
4. **Must not** be a copy-paste of the first sentence of the page body
5. **Must not** use generic fluff from the zero-pollution list (Part 2, Step 1) — no "high quality," "professional team," "best service"
6. **Should** use active voice and the buyer's own language (same as Part 7 tone rules)

**Before**:
> "We are a professional supply chain management company providing high-quality services to businesses worldwide. Contact us today to learn more about how we can help your business grow."

**After**:
> "Hidden supply chain costs bleed 15-30% of your margins. Our 3-stage QC inspection catches defects competitors miss — 340+ batches rejected in 2024 with full photo evidence. Get a free diagnostic in 24 hours."

**Quick validation**:
- [ ] 150-160 characters (check with SERP preview tool)
- [ ] Contains a specific number or quantified outcome
- [ ] Contains a unique differentiator (not claimable by any competitor)
- [ ] Ends with a low-risk action
- [ ] Reads as a natural sentence, not keyword-stuffed
- [ ] Distinct from Title — doesn't repeat the same phrasing

## Part 6: Entity Coverage (6 Dimensions)

Every landing page must cover at least 4 of these 6 entity dimensions. Covering all 6 puts your entity signal far ahead of 90% of competitors:

| Dimension | What It Covers | Where to Place | Common Missing |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Standards/Certifications** | ISO, SOC 2, FDA, GDPR, industry-specific cert numbers | Hero, Why Different, Trust Bar | Only writing "meets international standards" |
| **Parameters/Capabilities** | Speed, scale, capacity, compatibility, SLA, MOQ | What We Deliver, How It Works | No parameters at all — buyer must guess |
| **Scenarios/Clients** | Real customers, use cases, specific industries | Real Results, Who This Is For | Only "widely used in various industries" |
| **Process/Customization** | How it works, configuration options, custom dimensions | How It Works, FAQ | Only "custom available" with no specifics |
| **Comparison/FAQ** | vs competitors, vs in-house, common objections | Why Different, FAQ | No comparison content at all |
| **Quantified Evidence** | Numbers, percentages, timelines, verifiable forms | Real Results, What We Deliver | Claims with no numbers |

## Part 7: Professional Tone

### Core Rules
- Write for a busy B2B decision-maker
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Active voice. "We cut costs" not "Costs were cut."
- Specific > general. "47 plants" not "many facilities."
- Questions as headers engage buyers (use buyer questions from interview)
- Avoid exclamation marks except in testimonials
- Avoid superlatives (best, greatest, ultimate) unless third-party verified

### Buyer Voice Principle
Write the way your buyers talk. Use the exact phrases and problem descriptions from your conversion interviews. If a buyer said "I'm tired of chasing suppliers at midnight," use that language — don't sanitize it into "stakeholders seek improved vendor communication."

### Competitive Differentiation Tone
Default mode is factual contrast, not attack:
- *Yes*: "Competitors typically provide a single QC checkpoint at shipment. We provide three-stage inspection (incoming materials + mid-production + pre-shipment) with photo evidence at each stage."
- *No*: "Other companies cut corners on quality. We're the best."

### Recommended Structural Patterns
- **Questions as section headers**: "Still spending Friday nights reconciling inventory across 3 systems?"
- **Bullets with evidence**: Every bullet has at least one number, name, or verifiable action
- **Conversation-close paragraphs**: End each section with a bridge to the next (not "Learn more" but "Here's exactly how we do it:")

## Part 8: CTA Optimization

### 3 Core Principles

1. **Specific Value-Oriented** (not vague action): "Get your free risk assessment report" not "Contact us"
2. **Low Risk + Low Friction** (eliminate time/harassment concerns): "5 minutes, no sales pitch"
3. **Urgency + Scarcity** (reason to act now): "Custom quote in 24 hours"

### Templates by Position

**Hero CTA (highest impact — contributes ~40% of conversion)**:
- "Get a Free [Your Service] Risk Assessment + Custom Plan (5 minutes, no sales pitch)"
- "Book a 30-Minute Free Project Diagnostic — We'll Identify Your Top 3 Hidden Costs + Solution Path"

**Mid-Page CTAs (between sections)**:
- After Pain Points: "Want to know how severe your pain points are? Free self-assessment + expert interpretation within 24 hours"
- After Deliver/Process: "Get the Full 6-Step Collaboration Flowchart + Sample Contract Template + Typical Timeline"
- After Case Studies: "See How Similar Companies Saved 18-25% in Costs → Request Your Custom Case-Match Report"

**Final CTA (page bottom)**:
- "Submit Your Requirements → Receive a Complete Quote + Risk Control Plan + 3 Matched Options Within 48 Hours (No Obligation)"
- "Last Step: Leave Your Contact Info and We'll Send Your Custom Solution (With Full Parameters and Evidence)"

### CTA Copy Checklist
- [ ] Length: 5-12 words optimal
- [ ] Strong verb: Get, Book, Download, Lock In, Avoid (not "Submit", "Learn More")
- [ ] Includes quantification: "5 minutes", "24 hours", "18-25%", "3 matched options"
- [ ] Risk reversal: "Free", "No obligation", "Within 24 hours", "No sales pitch"
- [ ] Multi-channel: Button + "or message us directly on WhatsApp/WeChat"

### Risk Reversal Patterns
- Free diagnostic / assessment (delivers value before commitment)
- No-obligation quote with defined turnaround time
- Milestone-based payment tied to verified deliverables
- "If we can't deliver the promised outcome in [timeframe], [remediation offer]"

## Part 9: Section-by-Section Writing Guidance

### Hero (Conversion Contribution: ~40% — Optimize First)
- **H1**: Follow Title/H1 formulas from Part 5. One sentence = biggest pain + biggest quantified benefit + strong trust signal. Must establish "this page is for me" within 3 seconds.
- **Subtitle**: 1-2 sentences — pain point + solution preview with quantification
- **CTA**: Exact text from conversion interview or CTA templates (Part 8)
- **Trust bar**: 5-8 words with verifiable data (e.g., "4.8/5 from 89 clients | 98% retention | ISO 9001 + 3-continent local teams")
- **Upgrade tip**: Trust bar items must be independently verifiable, never "best in class"

### Pain Points
- Lead with 1-2 buyer questions from interview (use their exact words)
- List 3-4 specific pains. Each is a concrete scenario with numbers, not a generalization.
- *Before*: "Suppliers often cause delays and quality issues."
- *After*: "Last Wednesday, a critical batch arrived with 12% dimensional deviation, causing 11 hours of line downtime — estimated loss: $87,000."
- Close with bridge to solution: "We've helped 47 growing brands escape this firefighting mode. Here's how."

### What We Deliver
- 3-4 offerings. Each follows the three-part formula (Part 4).
- Every offering has a specific number, parameter, or timeframe.
- *Before*: "We provide reliable supplier sourcing and quality control."
- *After*: "Direct access to 90%+ of factories invisible to ordinary buyers (23 new long-term partner factories passed 3 rounds of rigorous audit in the past 12 months). Competitive bidding + open-book costing — average unit cost reduction of 12-28% for clients."
- **Upgrade tip**: Each deliverable must answer "What are the parameters/capabilities?" (Part 6, Dimension 2).

### How It Works
- 3-5 steps. Each step has: action + time + deliverable + client benefit + low-risk checkpoint.
- Visual-friendly (icon or illustration per step).
- *Before*: "Step 1: Discovery Call. Step 2: We find suppliers."
- *After*: "Step 1: Discovery Call (30 min, free) → We map your goals, current pain points, budget range, and key risk areas. You walk away with a preliminary diagnostic framework. Step 2: Custom Supplier Shortlist (7-10 business days) → You receive detailed dossiers on 3-5 pre-audited factories (cost breakdown, capacity proof, similar client cases, risk rating)."
- **Upgrade tip**: End the flow with "Milestone-based payment throughout — verification before each next phase."

### Our Team (optional — skip for solopreneurs/freelancers)
- Only include if team credentials are a key differentiator.
- Each person: 2-3 sentences of real background + specific quantifiable achievement + photo if available.
- *Before*: "John has 15 years of industry experience."
- *After*: "Sarah Chen — Head of Supplier Development. 11 years on Shenzhen factory floors, personally audited 200+ suppliers. Since joining, her supplier development program helped clients avoid $3.4M+ in potential quality and delay losses over the past 12 months — one automotive parts client reduced defect rate from 4.2% to 0.7%."

### Real Results (Strongest trust module)
- 1-2 mini case studies. Format: Challenge (specific pain + quantified impact) → Solution (with parameters) → Quantified Results (multi-dimensional) → Evidence Type (photo, report, client quote, timeline).
- Include client name/industry and specific timeline.
- *Before*: "We helped a client improve delivery times."
- *After*: "Home Goods Brand — $800K to $4.2M revenue in 14 months. Challenge: 19 active suppliers, 61% on-time delivery, hidden costs eating 19% of margin. Solution: Consolidated to 4 strategic suppliers, implemented open-book costing + multi-stage QC + real-time tracking, reserved 15% flex capacity. Result: 96% on-time delivery, +19 percentage points gross margin, $187K annual savings in rush/claim costs, weekly chase time dropped from 8 hours to 1.5 hours. Evidence: CFO-signed cost savings report + factory real-time photos + Q3-Q4 2024 delivery data screenshots."
- **Upgrade tip**: Prioritize counterintuitive or high-conviction data (e.g., rejection records, improvement from low to high).

### Who This Is For
- 3-4 customer profiles. Each profile: role + pain + why this solution fits.
- Use the buyer's own words as sub-headlines so they self-identify.
- Can use "This is NOT for" section to disqualify and focus.
- *Example sub-headline*: "We're growing fast, but our current suppliers can't keep up — constant delays and quality swings."
- Each profile ends with "Typical result: [quantified outcome]."
- **Upgrade tip**: Each profile must correspond to a specific scenario covered in Entity Coverage Dimension 3.

### Why We're Different (Highest concentration of information gain)
- Direct comparison with competitor weaknesses (from competitive calibration, Part 2 Step 3).
- Format: "Competitors do X. We do Y, which means [specific benefit]."
- Every difference must be verifiable (backed by data, case, process, or certification).
- *Before*: "We offer better quality control."
- *After*: "Most sourcing agents provide a single QC checkpoint at shipment. We provide three-stage inspection (incoming materials + mid-production + pre-shipment) with photo evidence at each stage. In the past 12 months, we actively rejected/required remediation on 340+ non-conforming batches and provided complete records and photos to clients. (Most providers never proactively disclose rejection data.)"
- Include at least 1 verifiable unique capability.

### What Clients Say
- 2-3 testimonials with: name, title, company, photo (if available).
- At least one testimonial must contain a specific number result.
- Format quotes to highlight the most compelling sentence; trim filler.
- *Before*: "They're great to work with."
- *After*: "Before, I spent several nights a week chasing suppliers. Now I can finally use that time to land new clients. In the past 12 months, our gross margin improved by 17 percentage points and quality complaints dropped to near zero. It's like having a reliable supply chain team embedded in your company." — Michael Torres, Founder, Verde Home (~$12M annual revenue home goods brand)
- **Upgrade tip**: Include both quantified results AND emotional transformation in the same quote.

### FAQ
- 5-8 questions drawn from buyer question list (Part 2, Step 2) — focus on questions not fully answered in the body.
- Answer format: direct answer (1 sentence) + detail (1-2 sentences) + evidence (optional).
- Must cover: minimum engagement/pilot options, payment terms, IP protection, SLA/remediation, competitor comparison, typical timeline.
- Include pricing question if pricing is known.
- End each answer with a bridge back to CTA where relevant.

### Final CTA
- Restate the primary value proposition (1 sentence).
- Primary CTA button (use templates from Part 8).
- Secondary CTA (link or smaller button — e.g., "or message us on WhatsApp").
- Optional: lead magnet offer below CTA.

## Part 10: Implementation Priority & ROI

### The 6 Highest-ROI Sections (Contribute 80%+ of Conversion Lift)

Rewrite these first, in this order:
1. **Hero** — 3-second first impression, ~40% of conversion contribution
2. **Pain Points** — Resonance locks the buyer in; if they don't feel understood here, they leave
3. **What We Deliver** — The "what's in it for me" with parameters
4. **Real Results** — Strongest trust module; proof that similar clients succeeded
5. **Why We're Different** — Information gain concentration; the reason they choose you over competitors
6. **CTA (all positions)** — The action vehicle; even perfect copy fails if CTAs are weak

### Tracking Metrics to Monitor
- Bounce rate, time on page, scroll depth
- CTA click rate per position (Hero, mid-page, Final)
- Form submission rate and average lead quality score
- Post-Google-indexing ranking changes

### Post-Launch 30-Day Iteration Cycle
1. Re-run the 5-Step SOP (especially competitive calibration) with fresh data.
2. A/B test different Headlines (pain-type vs. benefit-type vs. evidence-type) and CTA combinations.
3. Update with new client cases, new data, and new certifications as they become available.
4. Save the 5 AI prompts (Part 2, Step 5) as templates — re-run them for every optimization cycle.

### Competitive Calibration

- [ ] At least 1-2 unique information gain points highlighted that competitors don't have

### Evidence Escalation
- [ ] Every major claim uses the 5-part formula: Specific Action + Number + Time Anchor + Real Person + Verifiable Evidence (Part 3)
- [ ] Evidence hierarchy applied — highest available level used per section

### Information Density

### Title, Meta Description & H1

- [ ] Meta description follows "Core Keyword + Specific Value Proposition + Differentiator + Low-Risk CTA" formula (Part 5)
