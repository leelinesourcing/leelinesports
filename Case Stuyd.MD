# B2B Case Study Writing Guide  
**High-Quality Collaborative Standard**  
*Merged & optimized from Nuoptima-style (result-driven, data-attribution) + Manufacturing/Sourcing-style (challenge-first, peer-to-peer)*

---

### North Star
Every case study must feel like a **peer-to-peer conversation** between a seasoned expert and a stressed decision-maker (procurement, engineering, marketing, or operations leader).

**Tone principles:**
- Professional but human
- Confident without bragging
- Specific enough that the reader thinks:  
  > “This exact problem is killing me right now.”

The goal is not to impress. The goal is to make the right buyer feel understood and then show a credible path to the same outcome.

---

### Core Principles (Non-Negotiable)

1. **Specificity beats polish**  
   Real numbers, real constraints, real interventions > smooth corporate language.

2. **Show causality, not correlation**  
   Always explain *how* you know the result came from your work (CRM tagging, before/after measurement, independent tools, etc.).

3. **Story flow > vanity metrics**  
   Readers care about the problem and the decision path more than the final big number alone.

4. **One clear audience**  
   Write for the person who has the problem, not for your internal team or generic “buyers”.

5. **Consistency over creativity**  
   Follow the structure. Variation comes from the content, not the format.

---

### Recommended Structure  
**Use this order. Adapt depth based on industry, but do not change the sequence.**

#### 1. Title (Result- or Challenge-focused)
Choose one of the two proven formulas:

**Result-first (best for services / digital / high-ROI cases)**  
`Client or Category: $XXX Result at X.Xx ROI from [Method]`  
Example:  
`Cortavo: $403K Contracted Sales at 4.6x ROI from Organic + AI Search`

**Challenge-first (best for manufacturing / engineering / quality cases)**  
`How We Reduced [Specific Defect] from [Bad] to [Good] for a [Industry] Client`  
Example:  
`How We Reduced Aluminum Porosity Defects from PPM 230 to 12 for an EV Battery Housing Project`

#### 2. Client Profile (1–2 sentences)
**Mandatory format:**  
**[Industry + Country/Region]**, **[product/application or service type]**, **[key scale metric]**.

Examples:  
- A German Tier-1 automotive supplier specialising in aluminum housings for EV battery modules, producing 216,000 pcs/year.  
- A US-based managed IT services provider serving 25–250 employee businesses, with average lifetime value of $47K–$100K+ per account.

**3-second filter rule:** A reader in the same industry must immediately recognise themselves.

#### 3. The Challenge (Most Important Section)
This is the emotional and logical heart of the case study.

Must include:
- Exact pain metrics (PPM, defect rate, ranking position, pipeline value, rejection numbers, etc.)
- Business consequences (OEM threat, line stoppage, lost revenue, audit risk, sales cycle length)
- How long the problem had persisted
- Previous failed attempts (if any)

**Strong opening pattern:**  
“The previous supplier had struggled with porosity defects for six straight months. PPM stayed stubbornly above 200. The OEM issued a formal delisting warning and gave 90 days to fix it or lose the program.”

Write so the target reader thinks: “This is exactly what I’m dealing with.”

#### 4. The Strategy / What We Did Differently
Never say “we provided high quality” or “we have experienced engineers.”

Instead show:
- Concrete interventions
- Decisions that others usually skip
- Timing of involvement (DFM stage, month 1–3 foundation, etc.)
- Tools or methods that created the difference

**Recommended formats:**
- 3–4 deliberate strategic bets (with the conventional alternative rejected)
- Or clear chronological phases (Phase 1 Foundation → Phase 2 Content/Tooling → etc.)

#### 5. Results (Always use Before → After)
Split into two clear lines when possible:

**Technical / Operational Results**  
PPM: 230 → 12 | Average Google position: 42.6 → 9.2 | First sample pass rate: 100%

**Commercial / Business Results**  
Contracted sales: $0 → $403,330 | On-time delivery: 92% → 99.2% for 14 months | 8 new clients closed-won

Always state the measurement method (CRM-tagged, independent tool data, client system, etc.).

#### 6. Key Takeaways (Optional but Recommended)
3–4 transferable lessons.  
This section turns a single story into a reusable playbook.

#### 7. Soft CTA
End with a low-friction invitation:  
“Have a similar project? Tell us your specs →”  
or  
“Want the same system applied to your [industry]? Book a strategy call.”

---

### Handling Anonymous Clients (Very Common)

Use one of these three methods (in order of preference):

1. **Technical Detail Mask** (Primary)  
   Replace company name with hyper-specific parameters:  
   “A German Tier-1 automotive supplier… 216,000 pcs/year aluminum EV battery housings”

2. **Challenge-Focused Title**  
   Lead with the problem rather than the client name.

3. **NDA Hook** (at the very end)  
   “Client name and full project details available under NDA. Contact us for complete case information.”

Never invent details. Never use the real name without written permission.

---

### Language & Tone Rules

**Do:**
- Use real-looking numbers (never round dramatically)
- Prefer precise business language (CRM-attributed, contracted sales, PPM, on-time delivery)
- Show the decision logic (“We prioritised value over volume because…”)
- Keep total length 400–800 words for most cases
- Include 1–2 specific process or strategic differences

**Never:**
- Use vague claims (“best quality”, “lowest price”, “industry-leading”)
- Write in corporate zombie language
- Put pure results first without context (story flow matters)
- Mention competitors by name
- Use hype words (revolutionary, explosive, game-changing)

**Preferred vocabulary examples:**

| Prefer                          | Avoid                          |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| CRM-attributed / tagged at source | estimated / modelled          |
| Contracted sales value         | pipeline value (unless clarified) |
| Deliberate strategic choice    | innovative approach           |
| Still a client / expanded scope| thrilled to continue          |
| Buying committee / economic buyer | target audience             |

---

### Data & Evidence Standards

- Always show **Baseline → Target → Achieved** when possible
- Explicitly state how attribution was done
- Prefer client system data (CRM, QMS, ERP) over third-party estimates
- Mention independent measurement tools when used (Search Console, DataForSEO, CMM reports, etc.)
- Note the date of last results update

---

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Starting with results and forgetting the pain
2. Using only vanity metrics (traffic, rankings, impressions) without commercial outcome
3. Writing a perfect success story with zero friction or adjustment
4. Being vague about what was actually different in the approach
5. Using rounded or suspiciously clean numbers
6. Making the case study longer than necessary
7. Ending without a clear next step

---

### Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Title contains either a strong result number or a specific painful problem
- [ ] Client profile is specific enough for the target reader to self-identify in 3 seconds
- [ ] Challenge section contains exact metrics + business consequences
- [ ] Solution section shows concrete actions, not generic claims
- [ ] Results use clear Before → After format
- [ ] Attribution / measurement method is stated
- [ ] Language is precise and free of hype
- [ ] Length is between 400–800 words
- [ ] Soft CTA is present
- [ ] Client anonymity rules followed (if applicable)
- [ ] Results update date is noted (if ongoing)

### Quick Templates

**Title:** [Client]: $XXX Result at X.Xx ROI from [Method]

**Client:** [Industry + region], [service description], [key scale metric].

**Challenge:** [Specific starting position + business pain]

**Strategy:** [3–4 deliberate bets or phased approach]

**Results:**  
**Technical:** [metric] → [metric]  
**Commercial:** [revenue / clients / ROI]

**Key Takeaway:** [1–2 transferable lessons]

Have a similar challenge? →

#### Template B — Manufacturing / Sourcing Style (Challenge-driven)
**Title:** How We Reduced [Defect] from [Bad] to [Good] for a [Industry] Client

**Client:** A [Country] [Tier] [industry] supplier, [product], [annual volume].

**Challenge:** [Painful, specific details with consequences]

**Solution:** [Concrete interventions at design / tooling / process stage]

**Results:**  
**Technical:** PPM 230 → 12 | First sample pass rate 100%  
**Commercial:** On-time delivery 99.2% for 14 months | Program renewed

Have a similar project? Tell us your specs →

---

**Usage Recommendation**  
Publish one case study every 3–4 weeks.  
Alternate between industries and between the two templates to keep the portfolio balanced.

This guide is designed for collaboration. Follow it strictly for consistency. Deviate only when the story genuinely requires it — and document why.
